## Rules

### The Four Hurdles — Mandatory Ordering
A K-1 loss must survive four limitations, applied **in this order**: (1) basis, (2) at-risk, (3) passive activity, (4) excess business loss.

- Partner's K-1 instructions, exact text: "There are potential limitations on partnership losses that you can deduct on your return. These limitations and the order in which you must apply them are as follows: the basis limitations, the at-risk limitations, the passive activity limitations, and the excess business loss limitations." [Partner's K-1 Instructions (1065) (2025), Page 3]
- Shareholder's K-1 instructions, exact text: "There are potential limitations on corporate losses that you can deduct on your return. These limitations and the order in which you must apply them are as follows: the basis limitations, the at-risk limitations, the passive activity limitations, and the excess business loss limitations." [Shareholder's K-1 Instructions (1120-S) (2025), Page 2]
- Pub 925 states the same order as a numbered list: (1) adjusted basis of the partner's partnership interest or the shareholder's stock plus shareholder loans, (2) the at-risk rules, (3) the passive activity rules, (4) "The excess business loss limitation under section 461." [Pub 925 (2025), Pages 18–19]
- Schedule E instructions: "Apply the basis rules, at-risk rules, and passive activity loss rules to your loss on Schedule E." The excess business loss limit is figured separately on Form 461 and "will not be reflected on your Schedule E." [Schedule E Instructions (2025), Page 8]
- Other deduction-specific limits (e.g., section 179) generally apply **before** the at-risk and passive loss limitations. [Partner's K-1 Instructions (1065) (2025), Page 3; Shareholder's K-1 Instructions (1120-S) (2025), Page 2]

**Where each disallowed amount lives:**

| Hurdle | Form/worksheet | Carryforward |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Basis (§704(d) / §1366(d)) | Partnership Basis Worksheet in Partner's K-1 instructions; Form 7203 (S corp) | Suspended **per entity**, indefinitely, until basis is restored [Partner's K-1 Instructions (1065) (2025), Page 4; Shareholder's K-1 Instructions (1120-S) (2025), Page 2] |
| 2. At-risk (§465) | Form 6198 (per activity) | "Any loss from a section 465 activity not allowed for this tax year will be treated as a deduction allocable to the activity in the next tax year." [Shareholder's K-1 Instructions (1120-S) (2025), Page 3]; disallowed portion "must be carried over to next year" [Form 6198 Instructions (Rev. Nov 2025), Page 8] |
| 3. Passive (§469) | Form 8582 Parts IV–IX (Part VII column (c) = next year's prior-year unallowed loss) | Carried forward until passive income, special allowance, or complete disposition — see passive-activity-losses.md |
| 4. Excess business loss (§461(l)) | Form 461 | Added back to income and "carried forward to a subsequent year as a net operating loss" [Schedule E Instructions (2025), Page 8] |

### Hurdle 1 — Basis Limitation

**Partners (§704(d)):** "Generally, a partner may only claim their share of a partnership loss (including a capital loss) to the extent it doesn't exceed their adjusted basis in the partnership at the end of the partnership's tax year. Any losses and deductions not allowed can be carried forward." It is "the partner's responsibility to track and maintain" outside basis; the item L capital account is the partnership's books and "can't be used to figure the partner's adjusted basis." [Partner's K-1 Instructions (1065) (2025), Page 3]
- Basis moves, per the Partnership Basis Worksheet: **increases** for purchase price/cash and adjusted basis of property contributed, net increase in your share of partnership liabilities (item K1), and all income items (separately and non-separately stated); **decreases** for distributions, net decrease in liabilities share, nondeductible expenses, depletion, and losses/deductions. Basis can't go below zero; distributions in excess of basis are taxable gain (worksheet line 11b). [Partner's K-1 Instructions (1065) (2025), Pages 3–4]
- **Liabilities count for partners:** item K1 shows your share of nonrecourse liabilities, partnership-level qualified nonrecourse financing, and other recourse liabilities — "Use the total of the three amounts for figuring the adjusted basis of your partnership interest." [Partner's K-1 Instructions (1065) (2025), Page 12]
- When basis is insufficient, the disallowed amount is allocated **pro rata** across loss/deduction categories, and suspended items "are suspended and carried forward for use in the next tax year in which the partner has adjusted basis in their partnership interest available." [Partner's K-1 Instructions (1065) (2025), Page 4]

**S-corp shareholders (§1366(d)):** "Generally, the deduction for your share of aggregate losses and deductions reported on Schedule K-1 is limited to the basis of your stock and loans from you to the corporation... Any losses and deductions not allowed this year because of the basis limit can be carried forward indefinitely and deducted in a later year subject to the basis limit for that year." [Shareholder's K-1 Instructions (1120-S) (2025), Page 2]
- Stock basis is adjusted annually in this order: (1) increased by all income (including tax-exempt income); (2) decreased (not below zero) by distributions (box 16, code D); (3) decreased by nondeductible expenses; (4) decreased by losses and deductions. "You should generally use Form 7203, S Corporation Shareholder Stock and Debt Basis Limitations, to figure your aggregate stock and debt basis." [Shareholder's K-1 Instructions (1120-S) (2025), Pages 2–3]
- **Loan (debt) basis:** generally the balance the corporation owes you; distributions don't reduce loan basis, and loans you merely guarantee or co-sign give basis only to the extent you actually pay on the guarantee. Unlike partners, S-corp shareholders get **no basis from entity-level liabilities**. [Shareholder's K-1 Instructions (1120-S) (2025), Page 3]
- **Form 7203 trigger** — Schedule E (2025), Part II note, exact text: "If you report a loss, receive a distribution, dispose of stock, or receive a loan repayment from an S corporation, you must check the box in column (e) on line 28 and attach the required basis computation." [Schedule E (Form 1040) (2025), Part II] The instructions phrase it as: "If you are claiming a deduction for your share of an aggregate loss (or you receive a distribution, dispose of stock, or receive a loan repayment from an S corporation), check the box on the appropriate line in Part III, column (e), and attach Form 7203 to your return." (The basis-computation checkbox is at Part II, line 28, column (e) on the 2025 form.) [Schedule E Instructions (2025), Page 8]

### Hurdle 2 — At-Risk Limitation (§465, Form 6198)

- **When required:** "File Form 6198 if during the tax year you, a partnership in which you were a partner, or an S corporation in which you were a shareholder had any amounts not at risk... invested in an at-risk activity... that incurred a loss." [Form 6198 Instructions (Rev. Nov 2025), Page 1] Also flagged per activity: check Schedule E Part II, line 28, column (f) and attach Form 6198. [Schedule E Instructions (2025), Page 8]
- **You are at risk for:** "1. The money and adjusted basis of property you contribute to the activity, and 2. Amounts you borrow for use in the activity if: a. You're personally liable for repayment, or b. You pledge property (other than property used in the activity) as security for the loan." [Pub 925 (2025), Page 21]
- **You are generally NOT at risk for:** nonrecourse loans (except qualified nonrecourse financing secured by real property), amounts protected by guarantees/stop-loss agreements, and amounts borrowed from a person with an interest in the activity (other than as a creditor) or a related person. [Partner's K-1 Instructions (1065) (2025), Page 8; Form 6198 Instructions (Rev. Nov 2025), Page 1]
- **Qualified nonrecourse financing (QNR) — key for rental real estate:** "Qualified nonrecourse financing secured by real property used in an activity of holding real property that's subject to the at-risk rules is treated as an amount at risk." QNR generally is financing for which no one is personally liable, borrowed for holding real property, and loaned or guaranteed by a government or borrowed from a "qualified person" (a person actively and regularly engaged in the business of lending money, e.g., a bank). [Partner's K-1 Instructions (1065) (2025), Page 12; Pub 925 (2025), Page 22] On the K-1, "you may use only the amounts shown next to 'Qualified nonrecourse financing' and 'Recourse'" in item K1 to figure your amount at risk. [Partner's K-1 Instructions (1065) (2025), Page 12]
- Your at-risk amount is **reduced** by losses allowed in previous years, by distributions received, and by debt converted from recourse to nonrecourse; if it falls below zero, previously allowed losses are recaptured as income. [Pub 925 (2025), Page 23]
- At-risk limitations apply **per activity**; get a separate statement of income/expenses for each activity from the entity. [Shareholder's K-1 Instructions (1120-S) (2025), Page 3]

### Hurdle 3 — Passive Activity Limitation (§469, Form 8582)

Full rental-focused coverage (default rule, $25,000 allowance, MAGI phase-out, 7-day/30-day exceptions, real estate professional): see **passive-activity-losses.md**. K-1-specific mechanics:

- "Generally, PALs are subject to other limitations (for example, basis and at-risk limitations) before they're subject to the passive loss limitations." [Form 8582 Instructions (2025), Page 2 "Coordination With Other Limitations"]
- **Where K-1 amounts enter Form 8582:** Part IV is only for rental real estate activities with active participation (feeds Part I, lines 1a–1c); "Use Part V for all other passive activities" — passive trade or business activities, passive rental real estate that doesn't qualify for the special allowance, and non-real-estate rentals (feeds Part I, lines 2a–2c). [Form 8582 Instructions (2025), Pages 9–10]
- **Reporting flow for K-1 box 1 (1065):** report income/loss from activities you materially participated in, in Schedule E line 28 column (i)/(k) "after applying the basis and at-risk limitations on losses"; for passive box 1 losses, use Form 8582 to figure the amount for column (g); passive income goes in column (h). Box 2 (net rental real estate) is "a passive activity amount for all partners" unless the real estate professional rules apply. [Partner's K-1 Instructions (1065) (2025), Page 14]
- **Material participation — 7 tests** (satisfy any one): (1) more than 500 hours; (2) your participation was substantially all the participation by all individuals; (3) more than 100 hours and at least as much as anyone else; (4) significant participation activities totaling more than 500 hours; (5) material participation in any 5 of the 10 preceding years; (6) personal service activity with material participation in any 3 preceding years; (7) regular, continuous, and substantial participation on all facts and circumstances (not available at 100 hours or less). Spouse's participation counts as yours. [Pub 925 (2025), Pages 7–8]
- **Limited partners:** "If you owned an activity as a limited partner, you generally aren't treated as materially participating in the activity. However, you're treated as materially participating in the activity if you met test (1), (5), or (6)." You aren't treated as a limited partner if you were also a general partner at all times during the partnership year. [Pub 925 (2025), Page 8; Form 8582 Instructions (2025), Page 5] Limited partners also are not treated as **actively participating**, so no $25,000 special allowance for rental losses held as a limited partner. [Form 8582 Instructions (2025), Page 4]
- Whether a partner is a limited partner **for SE tax** is a separate question decided under section 1402(a)(13). [Partner's K-1 Instructions (1065) (2025), Page 2]

### PTP Special Regime — Losses NEVER Go on Form 8582
- "Section 469(k) provides that the passive activity limitations must be applied separately to items from each PTP. PALs from a PTP may generally be used only to offset income or gain from passive activities of the same PTP. The special allowance for rental real estate activities... doesn't apply to PALs from a PTP." And: "Don't report passive income, gains, or losses from a PTP on Form 8582." [Form 8582 Instructions (2025), Page 15]
- Same rule in Pub 925: "You can offset deductions from passive activities of a PTP only against income or gain from passive activities of the same PTP." [Pub 925 (2025), Page 4] A PTP is a partnership whose interests trade on an established securities market or are readily tradable on a secondary market. [Form 8582 Instructions (2025), Page 15]
- Mechanics per PTP: combine current-year items with prior-year unallowed losses. Overall gain → net gain is **nonpassive income** (also counts in MAGI for the Form 8582 special allowance). Overall loss → allowed only up to income; excess "is suspended and carried forward to be applied against passive income from the same PTP in later years"; identify entries "From PTP." Item D box checked on the K-1 = you're in a PTP. [Partner's K-1 Instructions (1065) (2025), Pages 10–11]
- **Complete disposition:** if you have an overall loss and "disposed of your entire interest in the PTP to an unrelated person in a fully taxable transaction during the year, your losses (including prior-year unallowed losses) allocable to the activity for the year aren't limited by the passive loss rules." [Partner's K-1 Instructions (1065) (2025), Page 10]

### Complete Disposition Rule (Non-PTP)
- "Any passive activity losses (but not credits) that haven't been allowed (including current-year losses) are generally allowed in full in the tax year in which you dispose of your entire interest in the passive (or former passive) activity. However, for the losses to be allowed, you must dispose of your entire interest in the activity in a transaction in which all realized gain or loss is recognized" — and the buyer must not be related to you. Any capital loss on the sale itself remains subject to the §1211 capital loss limit. [Pub 925 (2025), Page 17]
- For partnership/S-corp interests: "If you dispose of your entire interest in a partnership, the passive activity losses from the partnership that haven't been allowed are generally allowed in full... These rules also apply to the disposition of stock in an S corporation." [Pub 925 (2025), Page 18]

### SE Tax Interaction
- **Partnership (1065) box 14, code A** — net earnings (loss) from self-employment go to Schedule SE. "If you're a general partner, reduce this amount before entering it on Schedule SE (Form 1040) by any section 179 expense deduction claimed, unreimbursed partnership expenses claimed, and depletion claimed on oil and gas properties." If box 14A is a loss, "enter only the deductible amount on Schedule SE" — i.e., after the loss limitations above. [Partner's K-1 Instructions (1065) (2025), Pages 21–22]
- Whether a partner counts as a limited partner (whose distributive share is generally excluded from SE income) "depends on whether the partner is considered a limited partner under section 1402(a)(13)." [Partner's K-1 Instructions (1065) (2025), Page 2] The precise split of which box 14A components hit a limited partner is computed by the partnership; I cannot verify the components beyond this from curated sources — check IRS.gov (Form 1065 instructions).
- **Guaranteed payments** (boxes 4a/4b) are determined without regard to partnership income, are generally **not passive**, and are reported in Schedule E line 28, column (k). [Partner's K-1 Instructions (1065) (2025), Page 15]
- **S corporation:** "Your share of S corporation income isn't self-employment income and it isn't subject to self-employment tax." [Shareholder's K-1 Instructions (1120-S) (2025), Page 1]

### QBI Interaction — Limitations Apply BEFORE QBI
- QBI information arrives on the K-1 at **box 20, code Z** (1065) and **box 17, code V** (1120-S): "Section 199A information. Generally, you may be allowed a deduction of up to 20% of your net qualified business income (QBI)..." Figure the deduction on Form 8995 (if 2025 taxable income before QBI ≤ $197,300 / $394,600 MFJ) or Form 8995-A. [Partner's K-1 Instructions (1065) (2025), Page 29; Shareholder's K-1 Instructions (1120-S) (2025), Page 17]
- Only losses that survive the limitations count toward QBI: "When determining QBI or qualified PTP income, you must include only those items that are qualified items of income, gain, deduction, and loss **included or allowed in determining taxable income** for the tax year." A loss suspended by basis, at-risk, or passive rules is not allowed in taxable income this year, so it doesn't reduce QBI until the year it's allowed. [Partner's K-1 Instructions (1065) (2025), Pages 29–30; Shareholder's K-1 Instructions (1120-S) (2025), Page 17; mechanics of tracking suspended-loss vintages are in the Form 8995/8995-A instructions — not curated here; if needed, I cannot verify beyond this — check IRS.gov]
