## Rules

### Qualifying uses
- To qualify, you must use part of your home: exclusively and regularly as your principal place of business; exclusively and regularly to meet patients, clients or customers; as a separate unattached structure used in connection with your trade or business; on a regular basis for certain storage; for rental use (see Pub 527); or as a daycare facility. [Pub 587 (2025), p. 2]
- **"Rental use" is the §280A(c)(3) rental of the dwelling unit or a portion of it**, meaning renting out the home itself. [Rev. Proc. 2013-13, §2.04, p. 2] Pub 587 only says "see Pub. 527" [Pub 587 (2025), p. 2]. Pub 527 covers renting part of the property you live in [Pub 527 (2025), pp. 23–24]. An office used to manage rentals elsewhere falls under §280A(c)(1) (principal place of business), not "rental use." *Reading from the cited definitions, not a quoted rule.*
- **Exclusive use:** the area must be used only for the trade or business. Mixed personal and business use fails the test, except for inventory/sample storage and daycare. [Pub 587 (2025), pp. 2–3]

### Trade-or-business requirement
- "To qualify under the trade-or-business-use test, you must use part of your home in connection with a trade or business. If you use your home for a profit-seeking activity that is not a trade or business, you cannot take a deduction for its business use." Managing your own investments is the example that does not qualify. [Pub 587 (2025), p. 3]
- §280A(c)(1) likewise requires use as the principal place of business "for any trade or business." [Rev. Proc. 2013-13, §2.02, p. 2]
- None of the sources here (Pub 587, Pub 527, Rev. Proc. 2013-13, Schedule E Instructions) say when a rental real estate activity counts as a trade or business for this test. Whether a rental activity qualifies: *I cannot verify this — check IRS.gov.*

### Principal place of business
- A home office qualifies as the principal place of business if (1) you use it exclusively and regularly for administrative or management activities of your trade or business, and (2) you have no other fixed location where you conduct substantial administrative or management activities of that trade or business. [Pub 587 (2025), p. 3]
- A home office that is your principal place of business also affects commuting: travel between home and a rental property is generally nondeductible commuting "unless you use your home as your principal place of business." [Pub 527 (2025), p. 5]

### Simplified method
- The allowable area is generally the smaller of the actual area used and **300 sq ft**. With more than one qualifying business, the combined limit is 300 sq ft. [Pub 587 (2025), p. 11]
- **Scope:** the method applies to any **individual** who elects it for a "qualified business use" of a home: a §280A(c)(1) business use (including principal place of business), §280A(c)(2) storage, or §280A(c)(4) day care. [Rev. Proc. 2013-13, §§3.01–3.02, p. 3] The scope is not limited by which schedule reports the income.
- **"Rental use"** under §280A(c)(3) cannot use the simplified method; it must use actual expenses. [Pub 587 (2025), p. 11; Rev. Proc. 2013-13, §4.08(6), pp. 9–10]
- **Worksheets:** Pub 587 names three kinds of filer:
  - Schedule C filers use the worksheet in the Schedule C Instructions.
  - Schedule F filers use the Pub 587 worksheet.
  - Partners with certain unreimbursed expenses use the Pub 587 worksheet.

  [Pub 587 (2025), pp. 11, 24–25]
- Under the simplified method, no business portion of home expenses is deducted [Pub 587 (2025), p. 19]. A taxpayer who itemizes deducts qualified residence interest, property taxes and casualty losses on Schedule A, and cannot use them to reduce the business's gross income. [Rev. Proc. 2013-13, §4.04, pp. 5–6]

### Where to deduct
- Pub 587 gives these reporting paths:
  - Schedule C: line 30, with Form 8829 as applicable.
  - Schedule F: line 32.
  - Partners with unreimbursed expenses required by the partnership agreement: Schedule E line 28.

  [Pub 587 (2025), pp. 18–19]
- Form 8829 is for home office expenses on **Schedule C** only. [Form 8829 Instructions (2025), p. 1]
- **Single-member LLC or sole owner reporting rental real estate on Schedule E Part I:**
  - The line to use: Pub 587, Pub 527 and the Schedule E Instructions give no home office line or worksheet for this case. Line 19 ("Other") covers ordinary and necessary expenses not listed on lines 5–18, but it doesn't mention home office. [Schedule E Instructions (2025), p. 7] *I cannot verify this — check IRS.gov.*
  - Eligibility for the simplified method: it depends on the §280A(c)(1) test, per Rev. Proc. 2013-13 §3.02 above, not on the schedule.

### Mortgage interest and Schedule A (actual-expense method)
- Figure all mortgage interest as a personal expense first (Pub 936 limits), then put the deductible amount for the business home on the worksheet. Schedule A gets the personal portion plus interest on other homes, to the extent not deducted elsewhere (for example on Schedule E). [Pub 587 (2025), p. 22]

### Deduction limit
- The home office deduction cannot create or increase a net loss from the business; the excess carries forward. See `schedule-c-guide.md` (Home Office) [IRC §280A(c)(5)] and [Pub 587 (2025), p. 11] (simplified method gross income limitation).
