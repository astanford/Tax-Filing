# Estimated Tax Safe Harbor & Underpayment Penalty (Form 2210 / GA 500 UET)

## Source
- Instructions for Form 2210, Underpayment of Estimated Tax by Individuals, Estates, and Trusts (2025), IRS Catalog Number 63610I, rev. Feb 17, 2026 — pages 1–11
- Form 1040 Instructions (2025), Line 38 "Estimated Tax Penalty" — page 65
- Publication 505, Tax Withholding and Estimated Tax (for use in 2026), Chapter 2 — pages 24–25 — **forward-looking only**: this edition governs 2026 withholding/estimates, not the 2025 return
- Georgia IT-511 Individual Income Tax Booklet (2025) — pages 11, 14, 18–19

## Applicable To
- **Forms:** Form 2210 (Part I lines 1–9, Part II boxes A–E, Part III, Schedule AI); Form 1040, Line 38 (estimated tax penalty) and Line 26 (estimated payments); Form 1040-ES vouchers; GA Form 500, Lines 26 and 42; GA Form 500-ES; GA Form 500 UET
- **Workflow Steps:** Quarterly estimated payment planning, penalty check at filing, pre-filing audit, next-year (2026) payment planning

## Rules

### Quick Decision Flow (Penalty Check at Filing)
1. Compute 2025 tax after credits plus other taxes minus refundable credits (Form 2210 Part I, lines 1–4). [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Pages 2–3]
2. Subtract withholding (line 6). If the result (line 7) is **less than $1,000** → no penalty, stop, don't file Form 2210. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Pages 1–2]
3. If 2024 tax was zero (full-year citizen/resident, 12-month 2024 year) → no penalty, stop. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 1]
4. Required annual payment (line 9) = smaller of 90% of 2025 tax or 100%/110% of 2024 tax. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 1]
5. Compare each quarter's timely payments against 25% of line 9 (or Schedule AI amounts). Any shortfall in any quarter → penalty for that quarter, even if later quarters catch up. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Pages 1, 4]
6. Unless box B, C, or D applies, leave Form 1040 line 38 blank and let the IRS bill the penalty. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 1; Form 1040 Instructions (2025), Page 65]

### Who Owes the Underpayment Penalty
Exact text of the general rule [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 1]:

> "In general, you may owe the penalty for 2025 if the total of your
> withholding and timely estimated tax payments didn't equal at least the
> smaller of:
> 1. 90% of your 2025 tax, or
> 2. 100% of your 2024 tax. Your 2024 tax return must cover a 12-month
> period."
- The Form 1040 instructions state the same test from the return's side — you may owe the penalty if:
  - "Line 37 is at least $1,000 and it is more than 10% of the tax shown on your return, or
  - You didn't pay enough estimated tax by any of the due dates. This is true even if you are due a refund." [Form 1040 Instructions (2025), Page 65]
- **The penalty is figured separately for each installment due date.** "Therefore, you may owe the penalty for an earlier due date even if you paid enough tax later to make up the underpayment. This is true even if you're due a refund when you file your tax return." [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 1]

### Form 2210 Part I Structure — Current-Year Tax vs. Withholding
Part I (lines 1–9) computes the "required annual payment" [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 2]:
- **Line 1** — 2025 tax after credits: the amount from 2025 Form 1040/1040-SR, line 22. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 2]
- **Line 2** — other taxes: Schedule 2 (Form 1040) lines 4, 8 (additional tax on distributions only), 9, 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17a, 17c–17j, 17l, 17z, and 19. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 2]
- **Line 3** — refundable credits, entered as a subtraction: EIC, additional child tax credit, refundable AOC, refundable adoption credit, premium tax credit, fuels credit, §1341(a)(5)(B) credit. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 3]
- **Line 4** — current-year tax (lines 1 + 2 − 3, per the form).
- **Line 6** — withholding: Form 1040/1040-SR line 25d, plus Schedule 3 (Form 1040) line 11 excess social security. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 3]
- **Line 7** — line 4 minus line 6 (balance after withholding). "To determine whether the total tax is less than $1,000, complete Part I, lines 1 through 7." [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Pages 1–2]
- **Line 9** — required annual payment = smaller of the 90%-of-current-year figure (line 5) or the prior-year-tax figure (line 8); each required installment is based on line 9. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Pages 1, 4]

### $1,000 De Minimis — No Penalty, No Form
"You won't have to pay the penalty or file this form if either of the following applies." [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 1]
- "You had no tax liability for 2024, you were a U.S. citizen or resident alien for the entire year (or an estate of a domestic decedent or a domestic trust), and your 2024 tax return was (or would have been had you been required to file) for a full 12 months." [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 1]
- "The total tax shown on your 2025 return minus the amount of tax you paid through withholding is less than **$1,000**." [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Pages 1–2]

### The Safe Harbors — 90% / 100% / 110%

| Safe harbor | Requirement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Current-year | Withholding + timely estimates ≥ **90% of 2025 tax** | [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 1] |
| Prior-year | Withholding + timely estimates ≥ **100% of 2024 tax** (2024 return must cover 12 months) | [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 1] |
| Prior-year, higher income | **110% of 2024 tax** if 2024 AGI > $150,000 ($75,000 MFS) | [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Pages 1, 3] |

- Exact "higher income" language [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 1]:

> "Higher income taxpayers. If your adjusted gross income (AGI) for
> 2024 was more than $150,000 ($75,000 if your 2025 filing status is
> married filing separately), substitute 110% for 100% in (2) above."

- The Line 8 instruction restates it [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 3]:

> "Enter the 2024 tax you figured above unless the AGI on your 2024
> return is more than $150,000 ($75,000 if married filing separately for
> 2025). If the AGI shown on your 2024 tax return is more than $150,000
> ($75,000 if married filing separately), enter 110% of the amount of the
> tax computed earlier."

- **Prior-year harbor requires a 12-month 2024 return:** "If you didn't file a return for 2024 or your 2024 tax year was less than 12 months, don't complete line 8. Instead, enter the amount from line 5 on line 9" — i.e., only the 90%-of-current-year harbor is available. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 4]
- Joint-to-separate transitions: if filing jointly for 2025 but not 2024, line 8 is the sum of each spouse's separately figured 2024 tax; if filing separately for 2025 after a 2024 joint return, your 2024 tax is your share of the joint-return tax. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 4]
- Farmers and fishers (≥ two-thirds of gross income from farming/fishing in 2024 or 2025) substitute 66⅔% for 90%. Not applicable to this filer. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 1]
- The 1040 instructions' version of the exception: no penalty if the 2024 return covered 12 full months and 2025 withholding + timely estimates (lines 25d, 26, and Schedule 3 line 11) are "at least 100% of the tax shown on your 2024 return (110% of that amount if you aren't in the business of farming or fishing, and your adjusted gross income (AGI) shown on your 2024 return was more than $150,000 (more than $75,000 if married filing separately for 2025)). Your estimated tax payments for 2025 must have been made on time and for the required amount." [Form 1040 Instructions (2025), Page 65]

### Required Installments and 2025 Due Dates
- "For most taxpayers, this is one-fourth of the required annual payment shown on Part I, line 9" — i.e., 25% per quarter entered on line 10 of each column. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 4]
- Payment columns (due dates) on Form 2210 Part III, Section A [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 4; worksheet column headers, Page 7]:

| Installment | Covers payments | Due date |
|---|---|---|
| 1st — column (a) | through April 15, 2025 | **April 15, 2025** |
| 2nd — column (b) | April 16 – June 15, 2025 | **June 15, 2025** |
| 3rd — column (c) | June 16 – September 15, 2025 | **September 15, 2025** |
| 4th — column (d) | September 16, 2025 – January 15, 2026 | **January 15, 2026** |

- Weekend/holiday rule: a payment due on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday is timely if made the next business day. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Pages 1, 4]
- **Withholding is deemed paid evenly:** "For withheld federal income tax and excess social security or tier 1 RRTA, you are considered to have paid one-fourth of these amounts on each payment due date unless you can show otherwise." [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 4]
- Electing actual withholding dates instead: "If you treat withholding as paid on the dates it was actually withheld for estimated tax purposes, you must check box D in Part II and complete and attach Form 2210 to your return." [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 4]
- A 2024 overpayment applied to 2025 estimates is generally treated as paid April 15, 2025 (if the 2024 return was fully paid by then). Mailed payments count as of the U.S. postmark date. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 4]
- **January 31 shortcut:** "If you file your return and pay the tax due by January 31, 2026, include on line 11, column (d), the amount of tax you pay with your tax return. In this case, you won't owe a penalty for the payment due on January 15, 2026." [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 4]
- Ordering: "Your payments are applied first to any underpayment balance on an earlier installment even if you designate a payment for a later period." [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 4]
- If Section A, line 17 is zero for all payment periods, you don't owe a penalty (but boxes C/D/E still require filing the form or its page 1). [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 4]

### Penalty Rate and Computation
- The penalty is figured per underpayment, per rate period, "for the number of days that each underpayment remains unpaid." [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 4]
- The 2025 penalty worksheet applies **7% (0.07)** per annum — underpayment × (days ÷ 365) × 0.07 — in all four rate periods [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 7]:

| Rate period | Dates | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | April 16 – June 30, 2025 | 7% |
| 2 | July 1 – September 30, 2025 | 7% |
| 3 | October 1 – December 31, 2025 | 7% |
| 4 | January 1 – April 15, 2026 | 7% |

- You don't have to use the Form 2210 method: "There are different ways to figure the correct penalty. You don't have to use the method used on Form 2210 as long as you enter the correct penalty amount on the 'Estimated tax penalty' line on your return" — unless a Part II box requires filing. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 1]

### Annualized Income Installment Method (Schedule AI) — Lumpy Income
"If your income varied during the year because, for example, you operated your business on a seasonal basis or had a large capital gain late in the year, you may be able to lower or eliminate the amount of one or more required installments by using the annualized income installment method." Periods are cumulative (Jan 1–Mar 31; Jan 1–May 31; Jan 1–Aug 31; full year), each annualized; you must use Schedule AI for **all** payment due dates if you use it for any, check box C in Part II, and attach Form 2210 with Schedule AI to the return. Part II of Schedule AI annualizes self-employment tax per period. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 8; Part II, Page 10]

**Flag: accountant territory.** Schedule AI requires per-period books for income, deductions, SE earnings, credits, and AMT, and an error propagates across all four columns. It is worth the effort mainly when a large K-1 allocation or capital gain lands late in the year and the equal-installment method would create a penalty on the early quarters.

### Which Taxes Count in "Tax After Credits" (Form 2210 Lines 1–2)
Form 2210 line 2 pulls in the Schedule 2 (Form 1040) amounts listed above. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 2] For this filer the load-bearing ones are:
- **Self-employment tax** — Schedule 2, Line 4. Confirmed included: the Schedule AI Line 16 instruction says to include "the same taxes used to figure Form 2210, Part I, line 2 (**except self-employment tax**)" — the exception exists only because Schedule AI computes SE tax separately in its Part II. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Pages 2, 10]
- **Additional Medicare Tax (Form 8959)** — Schedule 2, Line 11 (see additional-medicare-tax.md). [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 2]
- **Net Investment Income Tax (Form 8960)** — Schedule 2, Line 12 (see niit-form-8960.md). [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 2]
- Also included via the line 2 list: additional tax on early distributions (Schedule 2, Line 8 — "additional tax on distributions only"), household employment taxes (Line 9 — but excluded if you had no federal income tax withheld and wouldn't be required to make estimated payments without them), and the Line 14–17/19 other-tax entries. Only the listed Schedule 2 lines count; the §965 net tax liability may be excluded from the required annual payment. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 2]
- AMT (Schedule 2, Line 1) is not in the line 2 list because it is already inside Form 1040 line 22 (Form 2210 line 1); Schedule AI's Line 16 instruction confirms AMT is picked up when annualizing. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 10]

### When the IRS Figures the Penalty / When Form 2210 Need Not Be Filed
- "Use Form 2210 to see if you owe a penalty for underpaying your estimated tax. The IRS will generally figure your penalty for you and you should not file Form 2210." [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 1]
- "Use the flowchart at the top of Form 2210, page 1, to see if you must file this form." [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 1]
- "If you didn't check box B, C, or D in Part II, you don't need to figure the penalty. The IRS will figure any penalty for underpayment of estimated tax and send you a bill. If you file your 2025 return by April 15, 2026, no interest will be charged on the penalty if you pay the penalty by the date shown on the bill. If you want us to figure the penalty for you, complete your return as usual. Leave the penalty line on your return blank; don't file Form 2210." [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 1]
- Part II boxes that force self-computation: "If box B, C, or D in Part II is checked, you must figure the penalty yourself and attach Form 2210 to your return" — B = partial waiver request, C = annualized income installment method, D = withholding treated as paid on actual dates. Box A (full waiver) requires only page 1; box E (joint return spouse-tax situations) requires filing page 1. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Pages 1–2, 4]
- Form 1040 side: "Because Form 2210 is complicated, you can leave line 38 blank and the IRS will figure the penalty and send you a bill. We won't charge you interest on the penalty if you pay by the date specified on the bill." And: "Don't file Form 2210 with your return unless Form 2210 indicates that you must do so. Instead, keep it for your records." [Form 1040 Instructions (2025), Page 65]
- **Waivers:** all or part of the penalty may be waived if (a) in 2024 or 2025 you retired after reaching age 62 or became disabled and the underpayment was due to reasonable cause, or (b) the underpayment was due to a casualty, disaster, or other unusual circumstance; federally declared disaster relief is generally applied automatically without filing Form 2210. [Form 2210 Instructions (2025), Page 2]

### Forward-Looking (2026 Planning) — Pub 505 (2026 edition)
*The following is from the Pub 505 edition "For use in 2026" — it governs 2026 estimates paid during 2026, not the 2025 return.*
- Same structure carries into 2026: pay the smaller of "90% of your total expected tax for 2026, or 100% of the total tax shown on your 2025 return" (the 2025 return must cover all 12 months); "Higher income taxpayers. If your AGI for 2025 was more than $150,000 ($75,000 if your filing status for 2026 is Married filing separately), substitute 110% for 100% in (2) above." [Pub 505 (2026), Page 24]
- 2026 installment due dates: April 15, 2026; June 15, 2026; September 15, 2026; January 15, 2027 — with the same file-by-January-31-and-pay-in-full shortcut for the final installment. [Pub 505 (2026), Page 25]

## Georgia Notes (Form 500-ES / Form 500 UET)
- **Who must pay GA estimated tax:** "Estimated tax is required for each individual subject to Georgia income tax who reasonably expects to have gross income during the year which exceeds (1) exemption for dependents, plus (2) estimated deductions, plus (3) $1,000 of income not subject to withholding." [IT-511 (2025), Page 14]
- **GA due dates** mirror federal: first filing by April 15; if the estimated tax requirements are first met April 1–May 31, file by June 15; June 1–Aug 31, by September 15; on or after September 1, by January 15 of the following year. Pay with Form 500-ES vouchers or electronically via the Georgia Tax Center (GTC). [IT-511 (2025), Page 14]
- **UET penalty:** "Failure to File Estimated Tax Penalty - 9 percent per year for the period of underpayment. Use Form 500 UET to calculate the penalty." Interest accrues separately at an annual rate equal to the Federal Reserve prime rate plus 3 percent. [IT-511 (2025), Page 11]
- **Where it lands on Form 500:** estimated payments (including Form IT-560 extension payments) go on Line 26; the penalty from Form 500 UET goes on Line 42 — "If you were eligible for an estimated tax penalty exception on Form 500 UET, please check the '500 UET Exception Attached' box, include the revised penalty on Line 42 of the Form 500, and include the 500 UET with the return." [IT-511 (2025), Pages 18–19]
- **GA safe-harbor percentages** (what fraction of current-year or prior-year GA tax avoids the UET penalty): not stated in the IT-511 booklet. I cannot verify this — check dor.georgia.gov (Form 500 UET and its instructions).

- **No W-2 withholding:** with no employer withholding, the entire payments section rides on timely quarterly 1040-ES and 500-ES payments — there is no "withholding deemed paid evenly across the year" cushion, so each installment must actually be paid by its due date.
- **Planning anchor — 110% of prior-year:** with K-1/rental/brokerage income that is hard to project mid-year, the 110%-of-2024-tax harbor (prior-year AGI > $150,000, MFJ) paid in four equal, on-time installments locks out the federal penalty regardless of how 2025 income lands; run the actual dollar amounts through a Python script, never in prose.
- **Georgia parallel:** make the matching 500-ES payments on the same four dates; the GA UET penalty (9%/year) is computed on Form 500 UET and entered on Form 500 Line 42.
